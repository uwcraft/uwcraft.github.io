Who do you trust inside your chips?

Cars, medical devices, aircraft and defence systems now run on commercial off-the-shelf electronics.

Behind every chip is a long chain of companies: designers, fabs, integrators, firmware programmers and distributors. Every link is an opening for IP piracy, cloning, tampering or sabotage.

In this exercise, you’ll face these risks from the inside, as the person who has to decide what to do next.

What is a tabletop exercise?

A game-based simulation. Your team faces a series of realistic crisis scenarios and decides together how to respond. There’s no technical setup and no real-world risk, just a safe, collaborative space to test your thinking, uncover gaps and practise decision-making under pressure.

How it works

The session runs in rounds. Each round puts your team in front of a different attack.

  1. Form your company

    Join a team of up to 10 participants and run a virtual company.

  2. Face the attack

    A supply-chain scenario unfolds, inspired by real cyber incidents. Your team decides how to respond.

  3. Debrief

    The presenter explains:

    • the story behind the attack
    • the real-world events and frameworks that inspired it
    • how industry and research counter it today

  4. Next round

    A new scenario brings a new type of attack.

What you’ll take away

  • Recognize

    the types of supply-chain attacks that target hardware and firmware IP.

  • Counter

    them with knowledge of current state‑of‑the‑art defences.

  • Assess

    the gaps in your own company’s practices and guidelines.

  • Understand

    the security models that keep a supply chain trustworthy.

CRAFT Cybersecure Robotics And Future Talent

A University of Waterloo program

Robots evolve faster than the security that protects them. CRAFT closes that gap.

CRAFT is an interdisciplinary education and training program at the University of Waterloo. It combines robotics, hardware, electronics and cybersecurity with hands-on learning.

How CRAFT teaches

  • 3-hour theory modules
  • Offensive and defensive approaches
  • Hands-on labs in automotive, hardware security and robotics
  • Tabletop exercises like this one

CRAFT is funded with $1 million from the National Cybersecurity Consortium. It is co-led by Dr. Yue Hu (Mechanical and Mechatronics Engineering) and Dr. Sebastian Fischmeister (Electrical and Computer Engineering). Its curriculum and materials are built to be shared with higher-education institutions across Canada.
